Interactive Drone Simulations of Dynamic Systems

This project explores dynamic systems through interactive drone simulations, using a swarm of small, agile drones as physical models. Real-time hand gestures, tracked via a webcam with MediaPipe and OpenCV, let users manipulate spring-mass-damper and pendulum systems modeled by differential equations, with responses visualized instantly through the drones' motion and live graphs.

Project Video

A Cooper Union summer research project, advised by Dr. Dirk M. Luchtenburg.

Project Details

Key Features

  • A swarm of small, agile drones simulates dynamic systems (single and double mass-spring-damper systems, pendulums) modeled by differential equations.
  • Users "grab" and move a drone by hand to set initial conditions, then release it to follow its differential equation model.
  • System responses are visualized instantly through the drones' motion and live graphs, with an optional overlay view that works without drones.
  • Constants like spring stiffness, damping, air resistance, length, and mass are all adjustable in real time.

Challenges & Learnings

The hard part was latency — getting hand tracking, the simulated drone, and the dynamic-system update to feel instant instead of laggy. That meant trimming the data pipeline and tightening the simulation loop until the delay disappeared. It also showed how much a math-heavy tool still lives or dies on how it feels to use.
