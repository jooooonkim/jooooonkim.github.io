Gear Train Race

A gear train built to lift a 10-pound weight as fast as possible, on a fixed torque budget. Three iterations later, it's modular: swap the gears, change the moment arm, change the tradeoff between speed and torque.

Final Product

Every component swaps out easily. Change the gears, change the moment arm — and with it, the tradeoff between velocity and torque the competition demanded.

CAD Design & Assembly

Design work ran through OnShape. Before anything got cut, the CAD model simulated how the parts would mesh, checked tolerances, and mapped out the assembly order.

Design & Fabrication Process

Iteration #1

  • The initial design was aesthetically pleasing but suffered from significant fabrication issues.
  • 3D printing imperfections resulted in measurement errors, creating unnecessary friction between gears.
  • The press-fit lid and base lacked sufficient strength and stability.
  • Not enough free space was left for the gears to turn freely.

Iteration #2

  • A modular design was implemented, allowing the moment arm to be easily replaced.
  • Swapped the support method from 3D-printed parts to stiffer metal shafts.
  • However, the unusual shape of the lid created inaccuracies in the 3D print, which affected gear alignment and functionality.

Iteration #3 (Final)

The final iteration fixed what broke in the first two. It has:

  • A truly modular design with components that are easy to replace.
  • Interchangeable gears to quickly adjust the moment arm, impacting velocity and torque.
  • A housing shape that prints accurately and keeps the gears aligned — no more warping.
  • A design that achieves optimal velocity and torque for the competition's specific requirements.
